The Problem

Every writer has started a book they never finished. The reason is almost never lack of ideas or talent — it is the gap between daily writing and a finished product. Substack made it easy to publish weekly posts. Amazon made it easy to sell books. Nothing connects the two.

The Five Phases

1. Concept

Define your book in a single outline. Four sections, four subtopics each, sixteen to twenty posts mapped. You know what you are building before you lay the first brick.

2. Schedule

Convert the outline into a twenty-week posting calendar. Each week gets a title and a one-paragraph brief. No more staring at a blank page.

3. Write

One thousand words a day. One post a week. Published on Substack, where readers follow along in real time. Your book is being written in public.

4. Curate

After twenty posts, the Truth Extractor scans everything you wrote and surfaces the core themes, the strongest arguments, and the gaps. The book that emerged may not match the outline — and that is fine.

5. Assemble

Posts become chapters. Transitions are written. Front and back matter are added. EPUB and PDF are built. A cover is designed. The book goes to Kindle.

The Daily Rhythm

  • Daily (1 hour) — Write 1,000 words toward this week's post
  • Weekly (30 minutes) — Publish, update schedule, preview next week
  • Monthly (1 hour) — Review all venues, advance what is ready
  • Quarterly (2 hours) — One book publishes. One venue starts its next cycle.

Five hours a week. One book every five months. Two books a year from a single venue.

Why It Works

Kindling replaces the heroic model of book-writing with a daily discipline. You never have to find a block of time. You write daily, publish weekly, and the book arrives as a natural consequence of sustained practice.

Your subscribers have been reading your book for five months before it hits Kindle. Launch day is a celebration, not a cold start.
