Our Story

How two lives with so many shared friends found each other.

"Two are better than one, because they have a good return for their labor."Ecclesiastes 4:9

Mark and Kim marry on October 12, 2026, at Sweet Heart Winery, surrounded by a small circle of family and friends. This is the story of how two full lives, lived for decades in the same corner of Colorado, finally found each other.

A Life Rebuilt

After Stacie died, Mark set out to build a new life. He threw himself into creative work and found real joy in writing and teaching. He invested first in his children and grandchildren, then in friendships through church. By the end of 2025, his days were full. He believed his best years were behind him and tried to be content with the good life he had already lived.

Before he ever met Kim, Mark wrote a description of the woman he hoped to marry: a woman of faith, deep kindness, and quiet strength, someone who listens with her heart. Then he made a decision. He would not chase that hope. He would wait until God brought her into his life.

In January 2026, wanting to meet more people his own age close to home, he joined eHarmony. The volume of strangers felt overwhelming, so he changed his goal from finding love to simply making friends. That is when he met Kim.

A Woman Worthy of a Song

Kim had spent her life moving toward people others overlook. Her compassion for the poor and the orphaned had taken her from the streets of Moscow to communities in the Philippines, Nicaragua, and Senegal. As a single woman in ministry, she often chose calling over comfort, and she did it without needing the spotlight.

Mark was amazed by her story. Kim builds up other leaders instead of collecting followers. She has friendships that span decades. She is bubbly and vivacious, yet grounded; adventurous, yet steady. "Some people live lives worthy of a song," Mark wrote that February. "She is one of them."

The surprises kept coming. The two of them shared a remarkable number of friends across the Christian community of Northern Colorado, people who had known each of them for years without ever introducing them. It felt less like chance and more like something supernatural had been at work all along.

Seeing Each Other Clearly

Through the spring, their conversations left both of them wanting more time together. Mark began to reimagine his future, and he wondered whether traveling together might show them what a life together could look like.

In June, they went to Macedonia for a CRU conference. There Mark saw Kim in her element: warm with strangers, curious, graceful in new places, turning every encounter into a real conversation. Kim later said that is when she feels most alive, when she walks into a room with one goal, to connect, and leaves people feeling encouraged and heard. The trip reignited that in her, and Mark got to watch it happen.

When they came home, Mark reread the description he had written of his future wife. It read like a description of Kim.

Yes

About a month after returning from Europe, Mark proposed, and Kim said yes. In the weeks that followed, they asked their friend Rick Olmstead, who had known them both long before they were a couple, to officiate. Mark's son Josiah would stand beside him as best man.

Mark has said there have been only a few times in his life when he knew with complete certainty that God was leading him. This is one of them. Kim's whole life has been a story of following where God leads, often to places no one expected. Now they follow together.

On October 12, at 5:00 in the afternoon, they make their promises: to build a home together, to listen to each other, to share every joy and every sorrow, and to keep choosing each other in all of it.

They are not looking back. They are beginning a new life together, and they can hardly wait to see what God will do with the two of them.
